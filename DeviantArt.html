"A Sudden Flight"
Written by Frank Jaspers on April 20, 2016.
A haiku with the pattern 5/6/6.

This haiku needs a fresh piece of art to accompany it. While it has had a couple of variation pieces in the past, I want to explore something entirely different for a future republication.

Addendum: English haiku count syllables (often 5/7/5; here 5/6/6), whereas Japanese haiku use 17 on with a seasonal kigo and a dividing kireji.

The form started as the hokku opening a group renga, until Matsuo Bashō (1644–1694) made it a Zen art of wabi-sabi.

The author's journals on Bashō's The Narrow Road to the Deep North explore more, and readers are invited to share haiku in the comments.
