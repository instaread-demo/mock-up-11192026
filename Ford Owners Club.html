The Mustang GTD 1901 Pays Tribute to Henry Ford’s First Race Car

Racing has been part of our heritage for as long as Ford Motor Company has existed. Technically, maybe even a little longer. So, with the 2028 model year Mustang GTD, 1 the latest evolution of the fastest factory Mustang ever made, we decided to honour one of the most important racing cars in our company’s history with the latest GTD livery, the 1901.

The 1901’s Royal Red paint and off-centre black stripe with a red centre line honour Henry Ford’s 1901 two-cylinder race car, built before Ford existed as a company.

Ford drove that car himself near Detroit and, as a novice, won $1,000, a punch bowl and publicity.
Its black body and red chassis inspire the new palette, joined by diagonal stripe graphics, exposed carbon fibre trim and a standard performance package.
Inside are Mahogany Red leather seats with Race Red stitching and seatbelt edging.

Every 2028 GTD now gets full-grain leather trim as standard.
Optional magnesium wheels save 22.8 kg of unsprung weight, and a painted stripe joins the options list.
An optional titanium package, 3D-printed from retired F-22 fighter jet parts, adds longer paddles, a new selector knob and a part-VIN dashboard plaque.

Scientific American praised the unconventional design of the 1901 “Sweepstakes” car weeks after its win.
Today’s GTD continues that spirit with a supercharged 5.2-litre V8 making up to 815 hp (826 PS), available active aero and extensive carbon fibre.

Joe Bellino is the Mustang GTD’s brand manager. 1 Homologated WLTP figures for the Mustang GTD: CO2 349 g/km and fuel 15.3 l/100 km, determined under Regulations (EC) 715/2007 and (EU) 2017/1151.
