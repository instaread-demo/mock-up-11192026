Remembering k.d. lang’s James Bond theme that didn’t quite make the cut

The news that Robert Pattinson has been cast as the next James Bond has made global headlines. It’s also prompted many other 007-related memories to pop up online.

One X user surprised many by posting about an often-forgotten Bond theme song by gay Canadian icon, k.d. lang.

Here is a look back at how that song came about, and how it ended up on the sidelines.

In 1997, lang was asked to record a theme for the Pierce Brosnan film Tomorrow Never Dies.

Composer David Arnold had impressed EON producers Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson with Shaken & Stirred, his 1996 Bond covers album, which he told Yahoo! Entertainment in 2022 he shared as a thank-you.

David Arnold chosen after Grammy win for Independance Day

Arnold’s scores for Stargate (1995) and Independence Day (1996) won him the job, and EON eased him in with the pre-title sequence.

His theme, “Surrender,” runs through the score, with lyrics by Don Black and queer singer David McAlmont. Arnold always pictured lang singing it, and says she nailed it in “two or three takes.”

k.d. lang: “I just thought the outcome was stellar”

Lang was proud of the demanding song, but producers moved it to the end credits and opened with Sheryl Crow’s “Tomorrow Never Dies,” which Arnold didn’t write. Lang told Yahoo she was disappointed; fans suspect Crow’s bigger profile won out.

Other contenders

The film was first called Tomorrow Never Lies until a typo changed it. Pulp’s rejected theme later surfaced as a B-side under that original name.

Duran Duran, Marc Almond and St Etienne also sent in songs.

Duran Duran reworked theirs into “Last Day On Earth” on Pop Trash (2000), while Swan Lee reportedly refused to hand their song to a better-known singer.

Blondie (“For Your Eyes Only”) and Radiohead (“Spectre,” passed over for Sam Smith) have also had Bond demos turned down.

Arnold’s Shaken & Stirred cover of “Diamonds Are Forever,” with David McAlmont channeling Shirley Bassey, reached No. 39 in the UK.
