From tarmac to APEC summit table, Japan's PM cuts a lonely figure

SHENZHEN, China, Nov 19 (Reuters) - From her arrival in China without the fanfare that greeted other world leaders to her seat near the end of a summit table, Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi cut an isolated figure at the two-day APEC summit.

Takaichi arrived on Wednesday on her first visit to China since triggering a dispute with President Xi Jinping last year by saying military action against Taiwan could prompt a military response if it threatened Japan's survival.

Beijing, Japan's top trade partner, has since curbed critical mineral exports; on Thursday Takaichi said Tokyo's Taiwan stance had not changed since 1972.

Trump got cheers and Xi's personal welcome, Albanese a bouquet and Marcos dancers; a ministry official met Takaichi.

She stood at the back row's far end in the group photo and sat seven seats from Xi, who was beside Trump and opposite Lee Jae Myung.

The pair shook hands twice but held no talks; Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara denied any snub.

Jiji spoke of a "cold shoulder"; Kyodo noted no bouquet-waving youths at her arrival.

Her only bilateral was a 30-minute exchange with Putin on a detained Japanese journalist and seized Japan Tobacco International assets.

Ending her 10th overseas trip, she named 14 leaders she had spoken with, not including Xi.
