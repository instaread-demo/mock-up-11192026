Trump, Xi and Putin sit down for a rare 3-way meeting in China, but appear to achieve little

By Joe Walsh, Tucker Reals

President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin sat down together in Shenzhen, China, on Thursday in a rare face-to-face encounter for the leaders of three of the world's most powerful countries, but there appeared to be little concrete to show for the meeting in terms of resolving any of the crises facing the world.

The roughly 90-minute session ended with a joint statement, and Mr. Trump flew home without taking questions, later posting that he would brief reporters Friday at noon.

The war in Ukraine came up, but the only real news came later, when Putin rejected direct talks with Kyiv over a Ukrainian strike this week on a Crimean bridge. Aide Yuri Ushakov called the mood good, and the Kremlin issued a joint statement the White House later shared.

It backed strategic stability, UN reform, more oil and gas output and the immunity of state officials, warned the International Criminal Court against overreach, and said nobody can win a nuclear war.

"A great relationship"

A Kremlin video showed Xi walking Putin to his motorcade as Putin thanked him and, per Russian media, said it went better than expected.

Earlier at APEC, Mr. Trump met Putin, then Xi, joking "Who is that?" before calling Putin a friend.

Sen. Tim Kaine, a Democrat on the Foreign Relations Committee, said the images troubled him and called the president either insincere or "deeply deluded" about Putin. On a call with NBC's Gabe Gutierrez, Mr. Trump gave the meeting a 12 out of 10.

Focus on Ukraine

TASS said Ukraine dominated the talks and Putin left in high spirits. He said Kyiv and Europe were not ready to compromise, ruled out talks after the Kerch Bridge attack, and predicted they would come around.

Kyiv and its European partners fear Washington could give Putin too much, including energy deals, while pushing Ukraine toward territorial concessions without security guarantees.

Mr. Trump also met Xi for about an hour, after two hours of trade and tariff talks on Wednesday, and said China should do more for American farmers.

Russia and the U.S. each hold well over 5,000 warheads; China has about 600. Both Putin and Mr. Trump let arms treaties lapse, and China, expanding its arsenal, refused to join talks last year.

Still, Xinhua quoted Xi urging a world without nuclear weapons, and the Kremlin pledged further dialogue on nuclear risks. Iran, trade and artificial intelligence also hung over the meeting.

Global trade and AI

Shenzhen's tech sector faces scrutiny over U.S. chips reaching China, a scandal tied to allegations about now-former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Alice Huang, whose firm, Megaspeed, is under Justice Department investigation and denies wrongdoing. Beijing calls the spying claims baseless.

Mr. Trump said he did not know that she was a spy, and his administration has restricted remote access to advanced AI chips.

At APEC, Mr. Trump vowed to block "globalist regulation" of AI and promised new trade deals. A tariff truce that followed the 2025 trade war, when tariffs topped 100%, is set to expire Jan. 10.

Iran, Taiwan and the case of two detained Americans also loomed over the talks.
