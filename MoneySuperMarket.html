Winter Fuel Payment scams: how to spot scam texts

Reviewed byAlicia Hempsted
Updated: 10 Nov 2026
Share

Pensioners are being warned to watch out for convincing Winter Fuel Payment scams as fraudsters exploit a time when millions are expecting to hear about help with their energy bills. Learn how to recognise the warning signs.

Key takeaways

  • Fraudsters are targeting pensioners with fake Winter Fuel Payment texts designed to steal personal and banking information.

  • Most eligible pensioners receive the payment automatically, so unexpected requests to apply, provide bank details or meet a deadline should raise alarm bells.

  • Don't tap links in surprise messages, keep your bank details private and send any dodgy text on to 7726.

The DWP says most eligible pensioners will be paid automatically in November and December, with confirmation letters now arriving.

HMRC has clawed the payment back from anyone with income above £35,000 since last year. Those who qualify get £100 to £300, normally into their State Pension account.

Scammers are cashing in as the Ofgem price cap is forecast to jump by up to £469 in January, lifting a typical bill to £2,192 a year1.

MoneySuperMarket Energy has found versions posing as the DWP, HMRC and other departments.

The texts say you have not applied yet or must hand over bank details, then set a deadline to reply or click a link.

Genuine payments arrive automatically and never require personal details by text or email.

Citizens Advice says over 7 million UK adults were scammed over the last 12 months2, while HMRC logged more than 25,000 such referrals in the year to April 2026.3

MoneySuperMarket’s top tips to protect yourself from scams

  • Ignore links in unexpected messages and check your eligibility on GOV.UK instead.

  • Never give personal, card or security details to unsolicited contacts; HMRC and banks won’t ask for them.

  • Keep devices updated, refuse remote-access requests and watch your statements closely.

  • Forward suspicious texts to 7726 and emails to report@phishing.gov.uk. See MoneySuperMarket’s Broadband guide for more.
