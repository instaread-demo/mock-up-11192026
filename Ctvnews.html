Canada’s religious landscape set for major shift by 2070: Pew

By Archie Niari

Published: November 18, 2026 at 12:39p.m. EST

Canada is expected to look considerably different on the religious map by 2070, with Christians projected to make up just one-third of the population and people identifying with no religion becoming the country’s largest group.

The forecast comes as Islam is projected to overtake Christianity as the world’s largest religious group within the next decade, according to new research from the Pew Research Center.

In Canada, Pew’s data tie the shift to waning Christian identification and growing non-affiliation rather than birth rates.

Canada’s changing religious landscape

Christians made up 53.3 per cent of Canadians in 2020, a share projected to drop to 33.3 per cent by 2070; the unaffiliated rise from 34.6 to 40.5 per cent.

Muslims are set to grow from 4.9 to 15.6 per cent, leaving the unaffiliated as the largest group.

Muslims projected to become world’s largest religious group

Pew counted nearly 2.3 billion Christians and about 2 billion Muslims globally in 2020; by 2035 Muslims lead, 2.57 billion to 2.52 billion.

By 2070: over 3.6 billion Muslims versus roughly 3 billion Christians, with world shares of 35.8 per cent (up from 25.6) and 29.1 per cent (up from 28.8).

Both groups still grow, Muslims faster.

Birth rates are a major factor

From 2020 to 2025, Muslim women averaged 3.1 children, Christian women 2.5 and women of other faiths combined 1.6.

Natural change adds about 1.6 billion Muslims and 792 million Christians by 2070.

Religious switching plays a role

Switching means a net loss of eight million for Islam and 105 million for Christianity.

Conversions between the two faiths roughly balance out.

What the projections mean for Canada

Across North America, Christians are projected to fall from almost 63 to about 46 per cent, the unaffiliated to rise from 30.2 to 41.2 per cent and Muslims from 1.6 to 4.9 per cent.

Pew notes the figures assume current trends in age, fertility, mortality, migration and switching continue.
