GTA VI is almost here, and you’ve probably pre-booked the game like me, maybe even preloaded it already. But the prep work isn’t done yet. Your TV might not be ready for Vice City the way Rockstar intended it to look. The good news is that a few tweaks is all it takes to make GTA VI look and feel the way it’s supposed to.

Switch to HDMI 2.1 and turn on VRR

The port you use matters the most

Only some of a TV’s HDMI inputs support HDMI 2.1, the standard a PS5 or Xbox needs. Over HDMI 2.0 it lifts refresh rate from 60Hz to 120Hz and bandwidth from 18Gbps to 48Gbps, and brings VRR and eARC, so connect the console to the port marked 4K/120 or eARC.

Next, switch on VRR (Variable Refresh Rate), usually under Display, HDMI or Game settings, so the TV follows the console’s output and tearing goes away.

Enable Game Mode

A no-brainer

Of the preset picture modes (Standard, Movies, Sports, Vivid, Game), Game is the one for play. It drops sharpening, noise reduction, contrast enhancement and other processing, each adding milliseconds of lag. Look in picture settings; it may be called Game Optimizer.

Turn off motion smoothing

Fake frames can cause real lag

Samsung’s Auto Motion Plus, LG’s TruMotion and Sony’s MotionFlow all insert guessed frames between real ones. With GTA VI capped at 30FPS that may appeal, but it adds input delay and the frames can look wrong. Game Mode often disables it; confirm under Display or Picture settings.

Don’t let power-saving settings ruin your game

Eco-mode and gaming don’t work well together

Eco modes, usually on out of the box, dim and cap brightness and throttle processing, leaving night scenes and neon streets dull for little real saving. Turn off Eco Mode or Energy Dashboard in General or System settings.

Get HDR right with HGiG

The HDR setting most people never find

Dynamic Tone Mapping suits films, but the console already handles tone mapping, so the TV repeating it can hurt the image. Disable it in Display or Picture settings and enable HGiG (HDR Gaming Interest Group) if offered: an industry guideline that maps HDR to your TV’s real capabilities for the most accurate picture.
