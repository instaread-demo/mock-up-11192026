Indonesia pitches 5.5mn tonnes of mangrove carbon credits at COP31 before any exist

Jakarta launched three blue carbon schemes in Antalya, led by a 43,000-hectare mangrove project in North Kalimantan whose credits are not expected to reach the market before late 2028. / Orpita Akther Ratna - Unsplash

Indonesia unveiled three mangrove and seagrass carbon projects at the COP31 climate talks in Turkey on November 17, but none has yet produced a credit buyers can trade, Kompas reports.

Deputy Environment Minister Diaz Hendropriyono launched the schemes, in North Kalimantan, on Java's northern coast and in East Belitung, at the Indonesia Pavilion in Antalya. "Today, on behalf of the Government of Indonesia, I am pleased to announce the launch of three blue carbon projects and initiatives," he said.

Jakarta is courting offset buyers, though this year's fires have renewed questions over credit integrity.

The 43,000-hectare flagship has about $4mn from the World Bank's M4CR programme, with Denmark as donor, Antara reports. Nani Hendiarti, a food ministry deputy, put its potential at about 5.5mn tonnes of CO2-equivalent over 40 years, pending validation.

That averages roughly 137,500 tonnes a year, yet no credits exist so far, and a sponsored Jakarta Post piece cited about 68,911 hectares.

Hendiarti said nearly half the economic value will go to coastal communities.

The fisheries ministry's two other schemes prioritise restoration: 81,000 hectares along Java's northern coast and 14,000 hectares around East Belitung, with director general Koswara stressing restoration first.

With 3.45mn hectares of mangroves plus 660,000 of seagrass, which officials put at roughly 17% of global blue carbon, Jakarta is seeking up to $260mn under its blue economy commitments. Only two of its 30 projects on show are certified.

On November 11, Norway signed up for as much as 12mn tonnes of PLN solar reductions, a deal analysts say could hurt credibility.

WALHI accused the government of breaking pledges, citing about 74,000 hotspots in 30 provinces.

How the flagship's estimate fares under independent checks may shape how buyers view the wider blue carbon push.
